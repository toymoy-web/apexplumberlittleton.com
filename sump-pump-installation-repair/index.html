Why This Matters More In Spring

Colorado's spring snowmelt combined with the clay soil common across Littleton means groundwater has fewer places to drain than it would in sandier soil, and that water finds its way toward basement foundations instead. A pump that's worked fine for years can still be caught off guard by an unusually heavy melt or a fast spring storm on top of already-saturated ground.

Signs Your Pump Is Failing

A pump that runs constantly, even when the pit isn't especially full, usually means a failing check valve or a switch that's stuck in the on position. One that never kicks on during a storm, when you can hear water entering the pit, points to a stuck float switch, a jammed impeller, or a motor that's simply worn out.

Unusual grinding or rattling noises during operation are worth addressing before the pump fails completely, since a motor that's already struggling rarely fixes itself.

What To Do If You Discover Flooding

Avoid walking through standing water near outlets, switches, or the electrical panel, and don't attempt to reach a breaker if doing so means stepping through water. Move valuables and stored items off the floor and away from the wet area first, then call, since a pump that's already failed won't fix itself while you wait.

Battery Backup: Worth The Add-On

A primary sump pump is useless during a power outage, which is exactly when heavy storms are most likely to knock out electricity in the first place. A battery backup system keeps the pump running for hours without power, and it's one of the more genuinely worthwhile upgrades for a Littleton basement that's ever taken on water before.

Repair Or Replace?

Usually Repairable

  • A stuck or misaligned float switch.
  • A failing check valve causing constant cycling.
  • A clogged intake screen restricting flow.
  • A tripped breaker or unplugged unit.

Time To Replace

  • A worn or grinding motor near end of life.
  • A pump undersized for the water it's handling.
  • No battery backup on a basement with prior flooding.
  • A pump older than 7 to 10 years with repeated issues.

Common Questions

How long do sump pumps typically last?

Most pumps last 7 to 10 years with normal use. One running constantly or making unusual noise is worth inspecting before it fails during the next storm.

Do I need a battery backup?

If your basement has ever taken on water, or you've lost power during a storm before, a battery backup is a genuinely worthwhile addition since a primary pump does nothing without electricity.

My pump runs constantly, is that normal?

No, constant cycling usually means a failing check valve or a stuck switch, not a sign the pump is simply working hard. It's worth having checked before it fails outright.

Yes. Our emergency line is answered 24 hours a day, which matters most exactly when storms are most likely to cause both flooding and power outages.
