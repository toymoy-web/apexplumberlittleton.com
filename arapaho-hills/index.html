Where Arapaho Hills Sits In Littleton

Arapaho Hills is one of the best documented neighborhoods in Littleton. The National Register of Historic Places listed the Arapaho Hills district on August 28, 2012. The City of Littleton's list of historic districts describes it as bounded by Arrowhead, West Berry and South Manitou Roads and South Lowell Boulevard.

A 2009 city reconnaissance survey tells the building story. Developer Edward Hawkins bought 160 acres in 1954. The plat filed on May 23, 1955 covered 37 acres and 83 lots, and houses went up between 1955 and 1964. The survey puts post-war modernism at the center of the architecture.

Verified Local Facts

Each card below is attributed to a source listed at the bottom of this page. Figures from real estate sites are aggregator numbers and not surveys.

Designers

Architect Joseph G. Dion planned the street layout and lots. Bruce Sutherland designed most of the houses, according to the survey.

Original Utilities

The survey says water and sewer service, streets, curbs and sidewalks were installed under Hawkins. It gives no further utility detail.

Condition In 2009

The survey describes a remarkable degree of integrity. Minor changes such as infilled carports and replaced doors or windows were common.

District Size

The survey proposed 56 contributing houses plus the sales office and carpentry shop. The city's List of Merit named 72 houses.

What Is And Is Not Documented

The survey places each house on a continuum between the International Style and the Usonian Style, using natural materials and earthtone colors. It documents architecture and not plumbing. We do not know the pipe materials, foundation types or sewer conditions of individual houses, and we will not guess them here.

Homes were built over nearly a decade with several designers and builders, so it would be unwise to assume one condition across the neighborhood. The only reliable answer for your house comes from inspecting it.

Plumbing Work In A Documented Mid-Century Neighborhood

General plumbing guidance, not a local finding. The points below apply to many Colorado homes. We list them because they fit what is known about this area, and we do not claim they describe your street.

Plan Access FirstBefore any finish is opened, ask where lines run and how the opening will be closed. Good planning limits repair to what is needed.
Find Leaks Before CuttingLeak detection can locate a hidden leak with little demolition. That matters when you want to protect original finishes.
If You Have A SlabSome mid-century houses sit on a concrete slab and some do not. We cannot say which houses here have slabs, but slab leak detection is a specific service if yours does.
Repipe After InspectionA whole-house repipe is a decision made after inspection and testing. A house of a certain age is not a reason by itself.
Match What ShowsVisible fixtures and trim shape the look of a design-led house. Talk through fixture choices before installation.
Exterior ChangesIf a project changes the outside of the house, ask City of Littleton historic preservation staff first. We have not verified the rules for your property.

Common Questions From Arapaho Hills Homeowners

Do you serve Arapaho Hills?

Yes. We are based in Littleton and serve this part of the city. Call (303) 552-3896 with your street address and we will confirm coverage.

Does historic district status limit plumbing repairs?

We have not verified the rules for specific properties. Ask City of Littleton historic preservation staff before any exterior work. They can tell you what applies to your home.

Do Arapaho Hills homes have galvanized or cast iron pipe?

The survey does not document pipe materials. Some houses may keep original lines and others may have been updated. A technician can identify the material in your house quickly.

Is leak detection better than opening the wall?

It is usually the better first step, because it finds the leak before any cutting. Any opening is then limited to the spot that needs repair.
