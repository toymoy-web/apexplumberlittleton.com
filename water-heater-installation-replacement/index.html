A water heater rarely fails without warning. Water heater installation and replacement in Littleton, CO usually starts with one of a few signs. Rusty water at the tap, a puddle at the base of the tank, or a unit old enough that waiting for it to fail is a bigger risk than replacing it on your own schedule are the three most common triggers.

Signs You Need Replacement, Not Repair

Rust-colored hot water, especially when it clears up after running the tap for a minute, points to internal tank corrosion rather than a fixable part. Once the tank itself is rusting from the inside, no repair stops that process.

A puddle or damp spot at the base of the tank means the tank has a crack or seam failure, and that's always a replacement, never a repair, since a leaking tank only gets worse. Rumbling or popping sounds during heating usually mean sediment has built up on the bottom of the tank, which is repairable early on but becomes a sign of a tank nearing the end of its service life if it's been ignored for years. Most tank water heaters are built for a 10 to 12 year service life, and a unit at or past that age is a candidate for planned replacement even if it's still limping along.

Choosing The Right Size

A 40-gallon tank covers most one to two-bathroom households. Larger homes, or ones with multiple showers running back-to-back, typically need a 50-gallon unit or a higher recovery rate to keep up. Colorado's cold incoming groundwater also slows recovery time compared to warmer-climate installs, which is a real factor in sizing a Littleton replacement correctly rather than just matching the old unit's capacity.

If running out of hot water has been an ongoing complaint rather than a new problem, that's also a good moment to compare against a tankless water heater, which solves the capacity question differently by heating on demand instead of storing a fixed supply.

What Replacement Actually Involves

Beyond swapping the tank itself, a code-compliant replacement often adds a few things the old installation may not have had. Common additions include a drip pan if the unit sits above living space, an expansion tank on a closed plumbing system, and updated venting or a sediment trap on the gas line. Which of these apply depends on your jurisdiction's adopted plumbing code, commonly UPC or IPC, along with the unit's age and location. We flag which of these your specific installation needs before starting, rather than after the old tank is already disconnected.

Repair Or Replace?

Usually Repairable

  • Pilot light or ignition issues on a gas unit.
  • A failed heating element on an electric unit.
  • A faulty thermostat or thermocouple.
  • Sediment buildup caught early, before tank damage.

Time To Replace

  • Rust-colored water from internal tank corrosion.
  • Any leak or puddle at the base of the tank.
  • Unit is at or past 10 to 12 years old.
  • Repeated repairs on the same aging unit.

Common Questions

How long do water heaters actually last?

Most tank units are built for 10 to 12 years of service. Water quality, usage, and maintenance can shift that range, but a unit at or past that age is worth planning to replace rather than waiting for a failure.

Is it cheaper to repair or replace?

A single repair on a younger unit is usually cheaper upfront. On a unit already past 10 years old, repeated repairs often cost more over time than one planned replacement, especially once the tank itself is corroding.

Do you haul away the old tank?

Yes, draining, disconnecting, and hauling away the old unit is included in every replacement.

Do I need a permit to replace a water heater?

Requirements vary by jurisdiction. We handle code-required additions like expansion tanks and venting updates as part of the installation, whichever your local jurisdiction requires.
