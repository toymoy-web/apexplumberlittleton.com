Hydro Jetting Vs. Snaking: Which Does Your Drain Need?

A slow or clogged drain has two common professional fixes, snaking and hydro jetting, and homeowners are often quoted one without understanding why, or whether the other option might actually be the better fit. Here's the real difference, in plain terms.

A Note On DIY Attempts

A basic household plunger or a small hand-crank auger can sometimes clear a very minor, shallow clog, and there's nothing wrong with trying that first for something simple like a slow bathroom sink. But once a clog is deep enough or stubborn enough that a basic household tool doesn't clear it, that's the point where professional equipment, not a stronger version of the same household tool, is the right next step.

What Homeowners Sometimes Get Wrong

It's a common assumption that a stronger chemical drain cleaner is a reasonable substitute for either professional method. In practice, chemical cleaners are far less effective against grease buildup, root intrusion, or scale than either snaking or jetting, and repeated chemical use can actually damage certain older pipe materials over time. If a store-bought product hasn't solved the problem after one attempt, that's usually the point to call a professional rather than trying a stronger product.

What Actually Determines The Right Choice

The single most useful piece of information is history. A drain you're dealing with for the first time is a very different situation from one you've called about three times in the past year. Repetition is the clearest signal that whatever's being done isn't addressing the real cause, and that's true whether the repeated fix has been snaking or even a previous jetting job that targeted the wrong section.

What A Camera Inspection Actually Shows

A camera run through the line reveals what's actually happening on the pipe walls: grease coating, root intrusion at joints, scale buildup, or even a genuine structural issue like a cracked or offset pipe section. This matters because jetting solves buildup problems extremely well but does nothing for a structurally damaged pipe, and pushing high-pressure water through a compromised section can occasionally worsen an existing crack. A camera inspection first isn't an upsell. It's what keeps the recommended service actually matched to the real problem.

What Snaking Actually Does

A drain snake, also called a cable auger, is a flexible metal cable fed into the pipe that either breaks through a clog directly or hooks it and pulls it back out. It's fast, effective for a single, localized blockage like a hair clog in a bathroom drain or a foreign object stuck in a kitchen line, and it's the right tool for a huge share of everyday residential clogs.

What Hydro Jetting Actually Does

Hydro jetting uses a specialized nozzle that sprays water at very high pressure, commonly around 4,000 PSI according to standard equipment specifications, through the full diameter of the pipe. Rather than punching a hole through a clog the way a snake does, jetting scours the entire interior pipe wall clean, removing grease buildup, mineral scale, and root intrusion material along the whole treated section, not just at the point of the blockage.

When Snaking Is Genuinely The Right Call

A single clog with a clear, recent cause, hair, a foreign object, or a one-time grease buildup, is usually a snaking job. It's less expensive, faster, and perfectly effective for a problem that isn't part of a larger pattern. If your drain has never given you trouble before and this is an isolated incident, there's no need to jump straight to a more intensive service.

When Hydro Jetting Is Actually Worth It

Recurring clogs in the same drain, despite previous snaking, are the clearest sign that snaking alone isn't addressing the underlying buildup coating the pipe walls. Root intrusion into a sewer line, grease-heavy kitchen lines in homes that cook frequently, and older pipes with years of accumulated scale are all situations where jetting's full-diameter cleaning genuinely outperforms a cable that only punches through the immediate blockage.

Jetting is also typically paired with a camera inspection first. Knowing the pipe's condition and the actual cause of buildup helps confirm jetting is the appropriate tool, rather than an oversized solution to a simple problem, or an underpowered one for a structural issue that jetting alone can't fix.

A Quick Comparison

Snaking Fits When

  • It's a single, isolated clog.
  • The drain has no history of repeat problems.
  • The cause is hair, an object, or minor debris.
  • You want the faster, less expensive option.

Hydro Jetting Fits When

  • The same drain clogs repeatedly.
  • Grease buildup is a recurring issue.
  • Root intrusion is suspected or confirmed.
  • A camera inspection shows scale along pipe walls.

Common Questions

Can hydro jetting damage older pipes?

Jetting is safe for most pipe materials and conditions, but a camera inspection first helps confirm the pipe is structurally sound enough for the process, since severely deteriorated pipe may need repair rather than cleaning.

Is hydro jetting more expensive than snaking?

Generally yes, since it's a more thorough process using specialized equipment. For a genuinely recurring problem, the more complete cleaning often prevents the repeat service calls that make snaking cost more over time.

Do commercial kitchens need jetting more often than homes?

Yes, grease volume in commercial kitchens accumulates faster, which is why regular jetting is often part of routine maintenance for restaurants rather than a reactive fix.

Will snaking make a recurring clog worse?

Snaking alone won't make it worse, but repeatedly snaking the same recurring clog without addressing the underlying buildup means paying for the same temporary fix again and again.

How long does jetting typically take compared to snaking?

Snaking a single accessible clog often takes under an hour. Jetting, especially with a camera inspection beforehand, is a more involved process that typically takes longer but addresses the full pipe length rather than one point.

Can I hydro jet my own drains with a rented machine?

Rental units exist, but professional-grade jetting equipment reaches higher and more consistent pressure, and a professional operator knows how to avoid damaging older or compromised pipe sections that a rental user might not recognize.
