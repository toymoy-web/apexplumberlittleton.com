Water Bill Up, But No Visible Leak?

If your water bill has climbed with no obvious cause, or you can hear water running somewhere with every fixture in the house turned off, that's exactly the situation leak detection is built for. Instead of opening up walls or digging up the yard to look, we use equipment that finds a hidden leak's location first, so any digging or wall work that follows is targeted rather than a guess.

Leak detection in Littleton, CO covers pipes behind walls, under slabs, beneath floors, and in yard or irrigation lines, using acoustic listening equipment and electronic correlation tools rather than opening up your home to search by hand.

How We Actually Find It

Acoustic leak detection listens for the distinct sound a pressurized leak makes as it escapes a pipe, which travels through the pipe material itself and can be picked up well away from the leak's actual location. For non-metallic pipe or noisier environments, correlator equipment compares the sound arriving at two points along the line and calculates the leak's position between them.

A water meter test, shutting off every fixture and watching whether the meter still shows flow, is often the first step, since it confirms whether the leak is on your side of the meter before any specialized equipment comes out. Any repair that follows still has to meet the pipe material and location requirements in your jurisdiction's adopted plumbing code, commonly UPC or IPC, not just stop the water.

The Water Meter Test

Shut off every fixture and appliance that uses water, then check whether your water meter's leak indicator, a small triangular or spinning dial on most meters, is still moving. If it is, water is flowing somewhere on your side of the meter even with everything off, which confirms a leak exists before any specialized equipment gets involved. This simple test alone has saved homeowners a service call when the culprit turned out to be a running toilet fill valve rather than a hidden pipe leak.

Finding Hidden Leaks In Littleton's Mixed Housing

A rising water bill with no visible cause is the classic sign of a hidden leak, and it can come from a slab, a wall, or a buried service line. Littleton's mix of Victorian-era blocks downtown and 1980s subdivisions means the likely leak location changes with the neighborhood.

Estate lots in Greenwood Village often mean longer buried runs from the street. Longer runs give a leak more room to hide, which is why we start with the water meter before opening any wall.

Can You Pinpoint It And Repair It Too?

Yes to both, and in that order. Detection comes first, and we mark the most likely spot so nobody opens a wall on a hunch. How closely a leak can be marked depends on pipe material, depth, and background noise, so the spot is confirmed before any cutting starts.

Some owners only want the location and plan to hire their own contractor. That is a fair request, and we will explain what we found in plain terms. If you want us to fix it, the repair is quoted separately once the leak is located.

What If Another Plumber Could Not Find It?

A leak that one visit missed is usually quiet, deep, or hiding behind a second problem. Tell the dispatcher what was already tried, such as a pressure test or a meter check. We start from that point instead of repeating it, and documentation of our findings is available for an insurer or a home sale.

Common Questions

Will you have to tear up my floor or wall to find the leak?

Not to find it. Electronic and acoustic equipment locates a leak without opening anything up. Some targeted access is usually needed to actually repair it once we know exactly where it is.

How do I know if I have a hidden leak?

An unexplained rise in your water bill, the sound of running water with every fixture off, or a warm or damp spot on a floor or wall with no obvious source are the most common signs.

Can you detect a leak in my irrigation system?

Yes, yard and irrigation line leaks are detected the same way as leaks inside the home, often showing up first as an unexplained soggy patch of lawn.

Is leak detection covered by insurance?

Coverage varies by policy and whether the leak has caused documented damage. We can provide documentation of what we find to support a claim either way.

What does leak detection cost in Littleton?

The price depends on where the leak is likely to be, how easy the area is to reach, and which method fits. Our dispatcher gives a clear quote before work begins, so you know the cost of the visit up front.

My water meter keeps running with everything off. Does that mean a leak?

It means water is moving somewhere past the meter. A running toilet fill valve is the most common simple cause. If the toilets are ruled out, a hidden line in a wall, slab, or yard is next.

How close can you pinpoint a leak?

Accuracy depends on pipe type, depth, and noise nearby. We mark the most likely spot and confirm it before anything is cut, so the opening stays small.

A plumber already looked and found nothing. What now?

Share what was tried. A follow-up visit that starts from those results saves time, and acoustic and correlator tools can find leaks that a visual check misses.

Can you check for a leak before I buy or sell a house?

Call before the inspection window closes if you can. We can test the supply side and the meter, then document what we find, which helps when a buyer or inspector raises a question.
