Tub-To-Shower Conversions

Converting an old tub to a walk-in shower is one of the more common remodel requests we see, often driven by accessibility needs or simply wanting a more usable daily shower over a tub nobody uses. The plumbing side involves relocating or adapting the drain location, since tub and shower drains often sit in different spots, and confirming the existing supply lines and valve can handle the new fixture's flow requirements.

Waterproofing: The Part Nobody Sees

A shower pan, whether pre-formed or built on-site, needs a properly sloped drain connection and a waterproof membrane that extends up the walls before any tile goes on. This meets the drainage and venting requirements in your jurisdiction's adopted plumbing code, commonly UPC or IPC. This is the single most common source of a "new" shower leaking within a year or two, and it's worth confirming with your contractor that this step isn't being rushed or skipped, regardless of who installs the tile itself.

Valve & Fixture Replacement

A single-handle valve replacement without full demo is possible in many cases if the existing valve body and rough-in location are still usable, which keeps costs down for a straightforward upgrade. A full reconfiguration, like adding a rain shower head or a second valve for a shower and separate tub filler, typically requires opening the wall to run new supply lines.

Matching Fixtures To The Rest Of The Bathroom

A shower valve, tub filler, and any exposed supply lines are worth matching in finish to whatever else is in the room. A mismatched finish is one of the more common regrets homeowners mention after a remodel is finished.

This is a design decision more than a plumbing one, but it's worth deciding before rough-in rather than after tile is already up, since valve trim kits are tied to specific rough-in valve bodies. Switching finishes later usually means replacing the whole valve, not just the visible trim.

Common Questions

Can you convert my tub to a walk-in shower?

Yes, this is one of our most common remodel requests. It typically involves relocating the drain and confirming the existing supply lines support the new fixture.

How long does a shower installation take?

A straightforward fixture or valve replacement can be done in a day. A full tub-to-shower conversion with new waterproofing typically takes several days, coordinated with tile work.

Do you handle the tile work too?

We handle the plumbing rough-in, valve, drain, and waterproofing membrane. Tile and finish work is typically coordinated with your remodel contractor or tile installer.

Why did my new shower start leaking after a year?

This is almost always a waterproofing membrane that wasn't installed correctly behind the tile, not a problem with the tile itself. It usually means reopening the wall to fix the membrane properly.

Can you match a shower valve to my existing tub fixtures?

We'll do our best to match finish and style, though exact matches years after original installation aren't always possible if the product line has been discontinued. We'll show you the closest current options.
