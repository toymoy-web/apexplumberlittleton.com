Where Sterne Park Sits In Littleton

Sterne Park is a city park, and it is also the name real estate sites use for the neighborhood around it. The Littleton Museum's park history places the park along Bemis Street and reaching east to Prescott Street and Lake Street. Homes.com puts the neighborhood just east of Historic Downtown Littleton, with Littleton Boulevard and Gallup Street as reference points.

No source we found draws a firm boundary around the neighborhood. If you are unsure whether your street counts as Sterne Park, it does not matter to us. We are based in Littleton, so call with your address and we will confirm we can come out.

Verified Local Facts

Each card below is attributed to a source listed at the bottom of this page. Figures from real estate sites are aggregator numbers and not surveys.

Park Origin

In June 1929 the Littleton Civic Commercial Association funded turning John B. Mayers Lake on Bemis Street from a swamp into a park.

Park Name

William C. Sterne contributed money to the project, and the park was named for him in appreciation of his support of Littleton.

Park Growth

South Suburban Park and Recreation District acquired more property in 1962, which extended the park to Lake Street.

Housing Figures

Homes.com lists a median year built of 1956 and a median lot of 9,583 sq ft. Treat both as aggregator figures and not a survey.

What The Homes Look Like

Homes.com describes ranch homes, bungalows, Craftsman homes and split levels on tree-shaded streets, and says many date to the midcentury period. Single-story houses are common in that description. We have not walked every street here, so we make no claim about pipe material or sewer condition for the whole area.

A build year alone does not tell you what is inside the walls. Repairs and remodels over several decades replace original materials in some houses and leave them in others. The reliable answer for your house comes from looking at the actual lines, and a technician does that on the first visit.

Plumbing Questions Worth Asking In A Mature Neighborhood

General plumbing guidance, not a local finding. The points below apply to many Colorado homes. We list them because they fit what is known about this area, and we do not claim they describe your street.

Slow Drains In Several FixturesWhen several drains slow down together, the main line is the likely place to look. A camera inspection shows whether roots, buildup or a damaged section is the cause.
Tree Roots And Sewer LinesRoots can enter a sewer line at a joint or crack. Mature trees near a line raise the odds, so ask about a camera check if backups repeat.
Water Heater AgeMany tank water heaters last roughly 8 to 12 years. The label shows the build date, and checking it early lets you replace on your own schedule.
RemodelsMoving a kitchen or bath often means rerouting drain and supply lines. Ask where the lines run before any wall is opened.
Uneven Water PressureLow pressure at one fixture is usually a local clog or valve. Low pressure at every fixture points toward the main supply or shutoff.
Hidden LeaksA rising water bill with no change in use can signal a leak. Leak detection finds it without opening large sections of wall or floor.

Common Questions From Sterne Park Homeowners

Do you serve homes near Sterne Park?

Yes. We are based in Littleton and serve this part of the city. Call (303) 552-3896 with your street address and we will confirm coverage.

Are Sterne Park homes old enough to have galvanized pipe?

We cannot say that for the area as a whole. Homes.com lists a median build year of 1956, but remodels and repairs change what is inside a wall. A technician can identify the pipe material at your house in a few minutes.

What does a camera inspection show?

A small camera on a flexible cable travels through the drain line. It can show roots, breaks, sagging sections or buildup, so the repair addresses the real cause.

Is Sterne Park the same as Historic Downtown Littleton?

No. Sterne Park is a separate named neighborhood and park east of downtown, according to Homes.com. We serve downtown addresses too, so a call settles coverage either way.

Who is responsible for the sewer line, me or the city?

Rules differ by place and by where the problem sits. In many places the homeowner maintains the service line up to the main. Confirm the rule for your street with the City of Littleton before assuming.
