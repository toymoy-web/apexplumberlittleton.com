Tap Water Tastes Or Smells Off?

If your tap water has a chlorine taste, a faint odor, or you've been buying bottled water because the tap just doesn't taste right, that's exactly what a filtration system addresses. Water filtration installation in Littleton, CO covers whole-home systems that treat every tap in the house and under-sink point-of-use systems focused just on drinking and cooking water.

Filtration Vs. Softening: Not The Same Thing

A water softener removes hardness minerals like calcium and magnesium that cause scale, but it doesn't address taste, odor, or chlorine. Filtration targets those different issues, chlorine taste and smell, sediment, and in some cases specific contaminants, and the two systems are often installed together rather than as a substitute for one another.

Whole-Home Vs. Under-Sink Systems

A whole-home system installs at the main water line and treats every fixture in the house, which matters if chlorine smell bothers you in the shower as much as at the kitchen tap. An under-sink system is more affordable and treats only drinking and cooking water at one location, a reasonable choice if taste at the tap is your only real concern.

Media Types And What They Actually Remove

Activated carbon is the workhorse media for chlorine taste and odor, adsorbing the chemical compounds responsible for both. Sediment pre-filters catch grit and particulate before it reaches finer media downstream, extending the life of more expensive carbon or specialty cartridges. Some whole-home systems layer multiple media types in sequence rather than relying on a single filter stage to handle everything.

Specialty media exists for specific concerns like iron staining or sulfur odor, both of which show up in some private wells more than in Littleton's municipal supply, but are worth mentioning if your specific complaint doesn't match a simple chlorine-taste profile. A water test, rather than a guess based on symptoms alone, is the most reliable way to match media to your actual water rather than buying a system built for a different problem.

Common Questions

Do I need a filter or a softener?

They solve different problems. A softener removes hardness minerals that cause scale; a filter addresses taste, odor, and sediment. Many homes benefit from both installed together.

Will a whole-home system reduce my water pressure?

A correctly sized system shouldn't cause a noticeable pressure drop. Undersizing for your household's usage is the most common cause of pressure complaints after installation.

How often do filters need to be replaced?

This varies by system and household usage, typically every 3 to 12 months depending on filtration media and water demand. We'll set expectations for your specific system at installation.

Can I just get a filter for drinking water only?

Yes, an under-sink point-of-use system is a more affordable option focused just on the kitchen tap if that's your main concern.

Does filtration remove lead from my water?

Standard carbon and sediment media aren't designed for lead removal specifically. If lead is a concern for your property's age or plumbing, ask about specialty media rated for that purpose.
