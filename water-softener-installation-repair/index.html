Salt-Based Vs. Salt-Free Systems

A traditional salt-based softener actually removes hardness minerals through ion exchange, which is the only method that truly "softens" water in the technical sense. Salt-free conditioners alter mineral structure so it's less likely to form scale, without actually removing the minerals, which some households prefer for sodium-conscious reasons even though it doesn't deliver the same slick, soft-water feel.

Repair Or Replace An Existing Softener

A softener that's stopped regenerating properly, shown by scale returning despite the system running, often means a failed control valve or a depleted resin bed. Resin beds typically last 10 to 15 years before losing effectiveness, so an older unit with declining performance is often a replacement conversation rather than another repair.

Where The Softener Fits In Your Plumbing

A softener installs at the point where the main water line enters the house, before it branches out to individual fixtures, so every tap benefits rather than just one. Most installations also include a bypass valve so the system can be serviced or bypassed entirely without shutting off water to the whole house, installed to meet your jurisdiction's adopted plumbing code, commonly UPC or IPC.

Repair Or Replace A Softener?

A softener that stops regenerating often has a failed valve, a clogged injector, or a control issue. A unit around six years old is often worth repairing. A much older one with a corroded tank may not be. We look at the parts and the age before suggesting either.

Installing A Softener You Already Bought

Many owners buy the tank first. The install needs a bypass valve, a drain connection, an electrical outlet, and a brine setup. Tell us the unit's grain capacity and model. Sizing depends on your water hardness and the number of people in the home, so the right capacity is not simply the biggest one.

Drain Lines And Damaged Connections

A regeneration line that was cut during a remodel stops the unit from flushing. A drain connection set too high can cause overflow. These are simple fixes when found early. If you are weighing a softener against whole-house filtering, see water filtration.

Common Questions

How do I know if I need a water softener?

Chalky scale on fixtures, spots on dishes after washing, dry skin and hair after showering, and reduced soap lathering are the most common signs of hard water in Littleton homes.

Salt-based or salt-free, which is better?

Salt-based systems actually remove hardness minerals and deliver the classic soft-water feel. Salt-free conditioners alter mineral behavior without removing them, which some households prefer for sodium reasons.

How long do water softeners last?

The resin bed typically lasts 10 to 15 years before losing effectiveness. The control valve and other components can fail sooner and are often repairable rather than requiring full replacement.

Will a softener help my water heater last longer?

Yes. Scale buildup from hard water is a real factor in reduced water heater and boiler efficiency and lifespan, and softened water measurably slows that buildup.

Do I need to keep buying salt forever?

A salt-based system needs regular salt refills, typically every 4 to 8 weeks depending on household size and water usage. A salt-free conditioner has no ongoing salt cost but works differently, as covered above.

Can you install a softener I bought online?

Yes, share the model and capacity. The install depends on space, a drain, and power, so the dispatcher will ask about those.

My softener stopped regenerating. What is wrong?

A failed valve, clogged injector, or blocked drain line are the usual causes. A technician can check each in one visit.

Do you service water conditioners and not just softeners?

Tell the dispatcher the brand and model. We confirm the visit first, so you do not wait for a tech who cannot help.

How big a softener do I need?

It depends on your water hardness and how many people live in the home. A hardness test and household size drive the sizing.
