A burst pipe doesn't wait for business hours, and neither do we. Emergency burst pipe repair in Littleton, CO means getting the water shut off and the damaged section fixed fast, whether the cause is a hard freeze, old corroded piping, or a sudden pressure spike.

Right Now: What To Do First

Shut off the main water valve immediately, usually located near the water meter or where the main line enters the house. This stops the flooding at the source while you wait for a technician, and it's the single most useful thing you can do before help arrives.

Turn off electricity to any affected area if water is near outlets or fixtures, and move valuables and electronics away from the water. Photograph the damage for insurance before cleanup begins if it's safe to do so.

What Actually Causes A Pipe To Burst

Freezing is a well-known cause in Colorado winters, since water expands as it freezes and can split a pipe from the inside, most often in exposed or poorly insulated runs near exterior walls or unheated crawlspaces. Our frozen pipe repair page covers that specific situation in more depth.

Corrosion in older galvanized piping, common in Littleton homes built before the 1970s, weakens pipe walls over decades until a normal pressure spike is enough to split a seam. A sudden pressure surge, sometimes from a water hammer effect when a valve closes fast, can also be enough to fail an already-weakened section.

Common Causes Compared

Freeze-Related

  • Most common in exposed or uninsulated pipe runs.
  • Often affects exterior walls or crawlspaces.
  • Prevention includes pipe insulation and heat tape.
  • See our frozen pipe repair page for winter-specific detail.

Corrosion Or Pressure

  • Common in pre-1970s galvanized piping.
  • Weakens gradually until a normal spike causes failure.
  • A pressure-reducing valve can help prevent recurrence.
  • Often a sign nearby sections may need attention too.

Preventing The Next One

Insulating exposed pipe in crawlspaces, garages, and along exterior walls is the single most effective step against freeze-related bursts. Keeping cabinet doors open under sinks on exterior walls during a cold snap lets warmer household air reach the pipe.

A pressure-reducing valve is worth checking if your home has consistently high water pressure, since that constant stress is what turns a normal pipe into a future failure point. If one section has already failed from corrosion, nearby sections of the same age and material are worth a look while we're already on site.

Common Questions

Where is my main water shutoff valve?

Usually near the water meter, in a basement, crawlspace, or utility closet where the main line enters the house. If you're not sure where yours is, it's worth locating it before an emergency happens.

Should I turn off electricity if there's flooding?

If water is near outlets, switches, or electrical fixtures and you can safely reach the breaker panel, yes. If reaching it means walking through standing water, wait for help instead.

Will homeowner's insurance cover a burst pipe?

Sudden, accidental bursts are typically covered under most homeowner policies, unlike gradual leaks or neglect-related damage. Check your specific policy and document the damage with photos.
