When Cabling Isn't Enough

If a drain snake cleared your clog last month and it's already back, that's usually a sign cabling only punched a hole through the blockage rather than actually clearing the line. Hydro jetting in Littleton, CO uses pressurized water, not a mechanical cable, to scour the entire inside diameter of the pipe, which is why it holds up longer against the exact problems that make cabling a repeat visit.

How It Actually Works

A jetting hose feeds a specialized nozzle through the line, spraying water at 4,000 PSI or higher both forward and backward as it's pulled through. The backward-facing jets pull the hose through the pipe while cutting through grease, sludge, and root intrusion along the entire wall, not just a narrow channel down the middle the way a cable does.

We run a camera inspection before jetting any line with unknown condition or history, since jetting a pipe with an existing crack or collapsed section can push debris into the damage rather than clear it. A camera afterward also confirms the line is genuinely clear, not just quiet for the moment.

Where It Works Best

Kitchen lines with built-up grease, main sewer lines with root intrusion, and commercial or restaurant drain lines all respond well to jetting specifically because the problem coats the entire pipe wall rather than sitting in one spot. A single isolated object clog, like a foreign item stuck in a toilet trap, doesn't need jetting and is better served by a simple cable or removal.

Cabling Vs. Hydro Jetting

Cabling (Snaking)

  • Punches a channel through the clog.
  • Faster and cheaper for a single, simple blockage.
  • Leaves grease and buildup on the pipe walls.
  • Root intrusion typically regrows within weeks.

Hydro Jetting

  • Scours the entire pipe diameter, wall to wall.
  • Clears grease buildup that cabling leaves behind.
  • Holds up longer against root regrowth.
  • Requires a camera check on unknown-condition pipe first.

Equipment We Actually Use

Trailer-mounted and truck-mounted jetting units both run the same nozzle technology, with the difference coming down to hose length and water tank capacity for longer commercial runs versus typical residential lines. Nozzle selection matters too, a penetrating nozzle designed to cut through a hard blockage first, then a chisel or root-cutting nozzle to clear the walls once the initial channel is open.

Hydro Jetting For Main Street Kitchens And Mature Lines

Restaurants and cafes along Main Street in Historic Downtown Littleton put grease and food solids into some very old lines. Jetting scours the full pipe wall, which a cable cannot do.

Residential jetting also helps in neighborhoods with mature trees, such as established blocks in Centennial, where roots and scale narrow older lines. A camera inspection first tells us whether jetting is the right tool or the line needs repair.

When Snaking Stops Short

Many callers reach us after a cable stalled partway down the line. A snake that stops at a fixed distance often hits a bend, a collapsed section, or a dense mass of roots. Jetting works differently because water pressure scours the wall instead of poking a hole through the middle.

Does The Line Need Jetting Or Repair?

That is the first question a camera answers. If the pipe is intact, jetting may restore full flow. If the pipe is cracked or sagging, jetting alone will not hold, and the plan shifts toward sewer line repair. We would rather tell you that up front than clear the line only for it to fill again.

Lines That Clog Every Few Years

A kitchen line that fills on a slow, repeating schedule is usually carrying grease, soap film, or roots. Jetting resets the pipe, and a camera shows whether the wall is smooth enough to stay clear. Ask about a maintenance interval that fits your pipe condition rather than a fixed calendar.

Equipment Questions Callers Ask

Customers often ask whether the truck carries a jetter before booking, and that is a smart question. State the line size and where the cleanout sits when you call. We confirm that the right tool is on the vehicle so the visit does not end with a second appointment.

Common Questions

Is hydro jetting safe for older pipes?

It's safe for sound clay, cast-iron, and PVC pipe. We run a camera first on any line with unknown condition, since jetting a pipe with an existing crack can worsen the damage.

How is this different from regular drain cleaning?

Standard cabling clears a channel through a clog. Hydro jetting scours the full pipe diameter, which is why it lasts longer against grease and root intrusion that cabling only pushes past.

Do you jet commercial or restaurant lines?

Yes, grease-heavy commercial and restaurant lines are one of the situations hydro jetting handles best, since the buildup coats the entire pipe wall.

Will jetting damage my pipes?

Jetting sound pipe is safe. The risk is jetting a pipe that's already cracked or collapsed, which is exactly why a camera inspection comes first on any line we don't already know the condition of.

My last plumber's snake got stuck far down the line. Can jetting help?

Often, yes, but a camera should come first. A stuck cable can mean a bend, a break, or heavy roots, and each needs a different plan.

Is jetting safe for old pipes?

It depends on condition. Sound pipe handles it well. Brittle or cracked pipe can be damaged, which is why we inspect before jetting.

Can you jet a kitchen drain line?

Yes, kitchen branches that carry grease are a common use. Tell us where the nearest cleanout is, since access affects the setup.

How much does hydro jetting cost?

The price depends on line length, access, and how heavy the buildup is. The dispatcher gives a quote before work starts.
