No Water At All Is Different From No Hot Water

If your whole house has no water at all, not just no hot water, or you've noticed an unexplained soggy patch of lawn between the house and the street, pay attention. The main water service line itself may be the problem rather than anything inside the house.

Water line repair and replacement in Littleton, CO covers this specific line, the pipe running from the city's connection point to your house, which is a different job from interior plumbing or a water heater issue.

Why This Line Fails

Littleton's expansive clay soil shifts with seasonal moisture and puts real stress on the water service line the same way it stresses sewer laterals. Older galvanized service lines are also prone to internal corrosion that gradually restricts flow before failing outright, which is why declining water pressure over months, not just a sudden failure, can be an early warning sign.

Trenchless Vs. Traditional Replacement

Trenchless water line replacement pulls a new line through the ground alongside or in place of the old one, avoiding a full trench dug the entire length of the yard. It works well when the existing line's path is known and reasonably clear of other buried utilities, and it's a genuine time and landscaping saver compared to full excavation.

A fully collapsed line, or one running through ground with other utilities crossing its path, sometimes still needs traditional excavation to do safely and correctly.

The Curb Stop Valve

Most properties have a curb stop valve near the property line, a shutoff point separate from the main valve inside your house, that lets utility crews or plumbers isolate your service line without affecting neighboring properties. If yours has never been located or tested, that's worth doing before it's needed in an actual emergency rather than during one. Any replacement line is sized and installed to meet your jurisdiction's adopted plumbing code, commonly UPC or IPC.

Service Lines From The Street To The House

The service line is the buried pipe between the meter and your home, and its age depends on when the street was built. Lines under Historic Downtown Littleton and the nearby older blocks are among the oldest in the area.

Roots, shifting clay soil, and freeze-thaw cycles all stress buried lines. Where the utility owns the pipe up to the meter, we tell you so before any digging starts.

Whose Pipe Is It?

In most places the water provider looks after the main and the meter. The owner is typically responsible for the line from the meter to the house. A utility crew that exposes a leak on your side still leaves the repair to you. Check your provider's rules, and call us if the problem sits past the meter.

Low Water Pressure Or No Water At The House

Start with the simple checks. See whether the main valve at the house is fully open, and whether the meter shutoff was left partly closed. A failing pressure regulator also drops flow everywhere, as does heavy buildup inside old galvanized pipe.

If the meter dial spins with everything off, the line is the likely cause. If it sits still and pressure is still weak, the problem is probably inside the house, and repiping may be the longer answer. When water is simply off, confirm the account is current and ask whether the street is under repair before booking.

Replacing An Old Line

Lead, galvanized, and early plastic lines are the ones owners most often ask about. Tell the dispatcher what material you have, or what year the house was built. A camera and a utility check show whether trenchless replacement is possible or a trench is needed.

Common Questions

How is this different from a sewer line problem?

The water service line brings clean water into your house; the sewer line carries wastewater out. A water line problem means reduced or no water pressure; a sewer problem means drains backing up.

Will you have to dig up my whole yard?

Not necessarily. Trenchless replacement avoids a full-length trench when the line's path is clear of other utilities. A camera and utility check tells us whether your yard qualifies.

Why is my water pressure declining gradually?

Gradual pressure loss over months, rather than a sudden drop, often points to internal corrosion narrowing an aging galvanized service line rather than a single failure point.

Is a soggy patch of lawn near the street a water line leak?

It's one of the more common signs, especially if the area stays wet in otherwise dry weather. It's worth having checked before the line fails completely.

How long does a water line replacement take?

Trenchless replacement is often completed in a single day. Traditional excavation can take longer depending on line length and landscaping restoration needs.

Can you replace the line from my meter to the house?

Yes, that is the section this page covers. The quote depends on the line's length, depth, and what sits above it, such as a driveway or a mature tree.

Why is the water off at my house when the bill is paid?

A closed valve, a failed regulator, or a broken service line can each cut flow. Check the main inside the house first, then ask the provider about street work. If neither explains it, call us.

Every faucet has weak pressure. What does that point to?

When all fixtures are weak, the cause usually sits before the branches split, such as the regulator, the main valve, or the service line. One weak faucet usually means a local clog.

Can you find the leak before digging up the yard?

Yes. Leak detection traces the sound of escaping water along the line, so the dig stays targeted. See our leak detection page for how that works.
