One Leak Is A Repair. Three Leaks Is A Pattern.

If you've had leaks show up in different parts of the house over the past year or two, or every faucet runs rusty or discolored water rather than just one fixture, that's a different situation than a single failed pipe. Whole-home repiping in Littleton, CO replaces the aging supply lines causing the pattern, rather than chasing the next leak wherever it shows up next.

Why Older Littleton Homes Need This

Galvanized steel piping, common in homes built before the 1970s, corrodes from the inside out over decades. The corrosion narrows the pipe's interior diameter, which is why low water pressure throughout the whole house, not just at one fixture, is often the first widespread sign alongside the rusty water and repeat leaks.

PEX Vs. Copper

PEX tubing flexes around obstacles during installation, which usually means less wall-opening than a rigid copper repipe requires, and it also resists freeze damage better than copper. Copper has a longer track record and a higher resale perception in some markets, but costs more in material and typically needs more invasive wall access to route.

How Much Wall Access Is Really Needed

A full repipe doesn't mean opening every wall in the house. Most repipes route new lines through basements, crawlspaces, and attics where possible, opening drywall only at fixture connection points, and we patch-ready those openings rather than leaving finish work to you.

PEX Vs. Copper Compared

PEX

  • Flexes around obstacles, often less invasive to install.
  • More freeze-resistant than rigid copper.
  • Generally lower material cost.
  • Newer material with a shorter long-term track record.

Copper

  • Decades of proven performance history.
  • Rigid installation typically needs more wall access.
  • Higher material cost than PEX.
  • Some markets perceive it as a resale value factor.

What Happens To The Water Heater

A repipe is a natural time to evaluate the water heater's connections too, since old shutoff valves and supply lines at the tank are often the same age and material as the rest of the failing system. We'll flag this during the estimate rather than as a surprise add-on once work has already started, so you know upfront whether it's worth handling in the same visit. Every new run is installed to meet your jurisdiction's adopted plumbing code, commonly UPC or IPC.

Common Questions

How do I know if I need a full repipe instead of spot repairs?

Leaks appearing in different locations over time, rusty water from every faucet rather than one, and whole-house low pressure together point toward a repipe rather than another one-off repair.

Will you have to open up every wall in my house?

No. Most repipes route through basements, crawlspaces, and attics where possible, opening finished walls only at fixture connection points.

PEX or copper, which should I choose?

PEX is often less invasive to install and more freeze-resistant. Copper has a longer track record. Both are valid choices depending on your home's layout and budget.

How long does a whole-home repipe take?

Most residential repipes take a few days to a week depending on home size, with water shut off only during active work periods, not the entire project.
