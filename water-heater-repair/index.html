No hot water rarely means the whole tank needs replacing. Emergency water heater repair in Littleton, CO usually comes down to one failed part, a tripped breaker, or a pilot light that won't stay lit, and most of those are same-day fixes. Our emergency line runs 24/7, so a cold shower on a Tuesday morning or a Sunday night doesn't have to wait until Monday.

Gas Unit Problems

A pilot light that won't stay lit usually points to a failing thermocouple, the small safety sensor that shuts off gas flow if it doesn't detect a flame. It's a straightforward same-visit repair on most units. A gas valve that clicks but doesn't ignite, or a burner assembly caked in soot, both show up as the same symptom of no hot water but need different fixes, which is why an actual diagnosis matters more than guessing at parts.

Electric Unit Problems

Electric units most often fail at one of two heating elements, upper or lower, and which one failed changes the symptom. A failed upper element usually means no hot water at all, while a failed lower element often still gives you a small amount of lukewarm water before it runs cold. A tripped breaker or a failed thermostat can look identical to an element failure from the outside, so we test before replacing parts rather than swapping the most likely part and hoping.

Is It A Leak Or Just No Hot Water?

A leak from a fitting, valve, or the pressure relief valve is usually repairable without touching the tank itself. A leak from the tank body, where water is seeping through the steel itself rather than a connection point, means the tank is failing and repair won't hold. If that's what we find, we'll walk you through water heater installation and replacement options instead of billing for a repair that won't last.

Before We Arrive: Gas Safety Checklist

If you smell gas near a gas water heater, don't attempt to relight the pilot yourself. Leave the area, avoid switches or open flames, and call from outside once you're clear.

Locate the gas shutoff valve on the supply line to the unit and turn it a quarter turn until it's perpendicular to the pipe if you're comfortable doing so. If you're not sure which valve controls the water heater, wait for a technician rather than guessing among multiple gas lines.

Gas Vs. Electric: Common Failure Points

Gas Units

  • Thermocouple failure is the most common no-hot-water cause.
  • Pilot light issues can also mean a dirty or misaligned burner.
  • Sediment buildup causes rumbling and reduces efficiency over time.
  • A gas valve replacement needs a licensed technician, not a DIY fix.

Electric Units

  • Upper element failure usually means no hot water at all.
  • Lower element failure often leaves some lukewarm water.
  • A tripped high-limit switch can mimic an element failure.
  • Breaker issues are worth checking before assuming a part failed.

Common Questions

Why does my pilot light keep going out?

A pilot light that won't stay lit is most often a failing thermocouple, the safety sensor that shuts off gas if it doesn't sense a flame. It's usually a same-visit repair.

Why is my water lukewarm instead of hot?

On an electric unit, this often points to a failed lower heating element while the upper element still works. On a gas unit, it can mean a partially failed thermocouple or a undersized burner flame.

Is a leaking water heater an emergency?

A leak from a fitting or valve should be shut off and addressed quickly but usually isn't a flood risk. A leak from the tank body itself can worsen quickly and is worth same-day attention.
