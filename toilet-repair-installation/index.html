Why Your Toilet Keeps Running

A toilet that runs constantly or refills itself every few minutes is almost always the flapper, the rubber seal at the bottom of the tank that's worn, warped, or not seating correctly. A fill valve set too high, or one that's failing internally, is the second most common cause, and it's why the water level in the tank matters as much as the flapper itself.

A toilet that rocks or wobbles at the base usually means the wax ring seal underneath has failed or the closet bolts have loosened. A failed wax ring lets water seep out at the floor with every flush, which can damage subfloor and flooring long before it's obviously visible, so a wobbly toilet is worth addressing before it becomes a bigger repair.

When It's A Clog, Not Just A Repair

A toilet that clogs repeatedly, even with normal use, can mean a partial blockage further down the line rather than a problem with the toilet itself. If cabling the toilet trap doesn't resolve it, that points toward the branch or main drain line, which is a drain cleaning issue rather than a toilet repair.

Installation & Replacement

A new installation includes setting a fresh wax ring, checking that the flange underneath is level and undamaged, and confirming the water supply line and shutoff valve are in good condition before connecting the new unit. An older or cracked flange gets flagged and repaired as part of the install rather than covered up under a new wax ring, since sealing over a bad flange just guarantees the same leak comes back.

Repair Or Replace?

Usually Repairable

  • Constant running from a worn flapper or fill valve.
  • A wobbly base from loose closet bolts.
  • A single clog cleared with a plunger or auger.
  • A slow-to-refill tank from a partially closed supply valve.

Time To Replace

  • A cracked tank or bowl, even a hairline crack.
  • Repeated wax ring failures at the same spot.
  • An outdated high-water-use model during a remodel.
  • Persistent clogging tied to an old, narrow trapway design.

Common Questions

Why does my toilet run every few minutes?

This is almost always a worn flapper not sealing fully, or a fill valve that's letting water past even when the tank is full. Both are usually same-visit repairs.

Is a wobbly toilet a big deal?

It's worth fixing promptly. A wobble usually means the wax ring seal has failed, and a failed seal can leak water under the floor with every flush, causing damage you won't see until it's more extensive.

How long does a toilet installation take?

Most installations, including removing the old unit and setting a new wax ring, are completed in under two hours.

Do you install low-flow or comfort-height toilets?

Yes, we install all major brands and styles, including low-flow and comfort-height models for remodels or accessibility needs.
