A Warm Spot On The Floor Isn't Nothing

If part of your floor feels noticeably warm even when the heat isn't running, or you've noticed a damp patch of carpet or flooring with no visible source, pay attention. That's one of the more recognizable signs of a slab leak. Slab leak detection and repair in Littleton, CO finds exactly where a leak sits under the concrete foundation before any concrete gets broken, using equipment built specifically for this situation rather than guessing at a location.

Why Slab Leaks Happen Here

Littleton's expansive clay soil shifts with seasonal moisture changes, and that movement puts real stress on copper supply lines running under a slab foundation. Hot water lines are especially prone to this kind of failure, since heat accelerates the corrosion process in copper pipe that's already in contact with shifting, moisture-variable soil.

How We Find It Without Breaking Concrete

Acoustic listening equipment picks up the specific sound pressurized water makes escaping a line under a slab, and thermal imaging can reveal a hot water line leak as a warm signature on the floor's surface. Neither method is visible to the eye otherwise, and combining both narrows the leak's location to a small, specific area rather than an entire room.

Repair Options: Spot Repair Vs. Reroute

A spot repair breaks a small, precisely located section of slab to access and fix the leak directly, which makes sense when the leak sits in an accessible spot like a garage or unfinished area. Rerouting the line entirely, running new pipe through walls or overhead instead of back under the slab, avoids breaking concrete at all and is often the better option under finished living space where slab access would mean tearing up flooring. Either approach has to meet the pipe material and support requirements in your jurisdiction's adopted plumbing code, commonly UPC or IPC.

Spot Repair Vs. Reroute

Spot Repair

  • Best when the leak sits in an accessible, unfinished area.
  • Involves breaking a small, targeted section of slab.
  • Faster when flooring restoration isn't a major factor.
  • Repairs the existing line rather than replacing its path.

Reroute

  • Avoids breaking concrete under finished flooring.
  • Runs new pipe through walls or overhead instead.
  • Often preferred under tile, hardwood, or carpeted rooms.
  • Can address an aging line's whole run, not just one leak.

Common Questions

How do I know if I have a slab leak?

A warm spot on the floor, the sound of running water with everything off, an unexplained rise in your water bill, or damp flooring with no visible source are the most common signs.

Will you have to break up my floor to find it?

No, acoustic and thermal detection locate the leak first. Some concrete access is typically needed to actually repair it, but only after the location is confirmed.

Is spot repair or reroute better?

It depends on the leak's location. An accessible spot in a garage or unfinished area often favors spot repair; a leak under finished living space often favors rerouting to avoid tearing up flooring.

Is a slab leak covered by insurance?

Coverage varies by policy, particularly around gradual versus sudden damage. We provide documentation of our findings to support a claim either way.
