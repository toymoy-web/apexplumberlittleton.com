Why Boilers Are Still Common In Littleton

Radiant and hydronic heating circulates hot water through baseboard radiators or in-floor tubing instead of blowing warm air. It was a common choice in homes built through the mid-20th century, and Littleton's older neighborhoods near Historic Downtown still have plenty of these original systems in place. A well-maintained boiler can run for decades, which is exactly why homeowners are often unsure whether a strange noise or a pressure drop means a quick fix or the end of the system's life.

The Kettling Noise: What It Actually Means

A banging, whistling, or rumbling sound during heating, often called kettling, is caused by mineral scale building up inside the heat exchanger and restricting water flow. Front Range hard water makes this a genuinely common complaint in Littleton, and the buildup forces trapped water to overheat into steam pockets, which is where the kettle-like noise comes from. Left alone, kettling accelerates wear on the heat exchanger, but a system flush usually resolves it if it's caught before serious damage sets in.

Other Common Boiler Problems

Low system pressure, usually shown on the boiler's built-in gauge, often means a small leak somewhere in the system or a faulty pressure relief valve. That's also why radiators can run cold at the top even when the boiler itself is firing normally. A failed circulator pump stops hot water from moving through the system entirely, which can look identical to a no-heat ignition failure until it's actually tested.

Pilot light and ignition problems affect boilers the same way they affect gas water heaters, most often a failing thermocouple or a dirty flame sensor. We check pressure, circulation, and ignition as a set rather than guessing at one part, since two of these problems can produce the exact same cold-radiator symptom.

Boiler Vs. Furnace: Know Which You Have

Boiler (Hydronic)

  • Heats water, circulated through radiators or floor tubing.
  • Common in Littleton's older, pre-1970s homes.
  • Kettling and pressure issues are the common failure signs.
  • Well-maintained systems often run for decades.

Furnace (Forced Air)

  • Heats and blows air through ductwork.
  • More common in newer Littleton construction.
  • No circulating water, so no kettling or pressure gauge.
  • A separate system, not covered on this page.

Common Questions

Why is my boiler making a banging or kettling noise?

This is almost always mineral scale restricting flow inside the heat exchanger, common with Front Range hard water. A system flush usually resolves it if caught early.

How long do boilers typically last?

A well-maintained hydronic boiler can run for decades, well beyond the typical 10 to 12 year life of a tank water heater. Age alone isn't usually a reason to replace one that's still running well.

Is no heat an emergency in a Colorado winter?

Yes. A no-heat call gets same-day priority year-round, and our emergency line is answered 24 hours a day for exactly this situation.
